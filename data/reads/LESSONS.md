# LESSONS — the ledger (docs/LEARNING.md)

Each lesson is asked as a question, then answered in his words. Only YES
lessons enter the morning read. A NO stays here so it is never asked again.

## 2026-10-26 — YES (taught by him, unasked)

- Incident: Southwell 7:00, a 6f novice on the all-weather, a race of
  lightly raced 3yos. Banked Big Money as a spot at 8/11, "the only runner
  with solid placed form". He was beaten by Prosewriter, a debutant (Archie
  Watson, Billy Loughnane), at 10/3. I had named Prosewriter as the next best
  and dismissed him as "a debutant".
- His words: "important lesson to be learned here, a three year old race on
  the aw, i would never touch it, never mind back an odds on, there is plenty
  of value down the field in these races."
- In the read: no banker and no spot in a 3yo novice or maiden race on the
  all-weather, and never an odds-on pick in one. Two or three runs of form is
  not enough to make a horse clear when unraced and lightly raced rivals can
  improve past him. The value in these races sits down the field.

## 2026-10-26 — YES (asked, answered the same night)

- Incidents: Siwa Oasis (Kempton 5:45) — I self-graded him NO BET at 13:15 because he had never won off his new mark (up 5lb to 82). He won at 2/1, "improved over 2f out ... kept on well". Fight Your Corner (Kempton 6:45) failed my 60-day race-fit line at 68 days and won at 4/1.
- The gap: both times I applied one line of the solid test strictly and overrode the stronger evidence beside it: the young-improver rule (3g-ii) and a C&D horse only just over the line.
- Question for William: **when a horse fails one part of the five-part solid test only narrowly (a race-fit 3yo improver up 5lb or less, or a course-and-distance horse a few days past 60), should the read weigh the whole case rather than rule him out on that one part? Yes or no.**
- **ANSWERED YES, 26 Oct, in his words:** "remember, 5lb rise. Remember I said to you about that? A three-year-old, that horse will be improving. He will only get better. So 5lb could be very, very doable. Look at his past victories. Look at the comments. Did he win well? Did he win going away? Was he eased down? These are all nuances that you can easily read and assess."
- In the read: a 3yo raised up to about 5lb after a win is not ruled out on "proven at mark". Read HOW he won. "Won going away", "eased down", "cosily", "readily", "easily" or "won well" mean more in hand than the margin, so the rise is likely within him. "All out", "just held on" or "driven out" mean little left. Weigh the whole case; never rule out on one narrow fail.
- His follow-up, same night: "it was there in black and white in his comment ... he was a horse getting better. A young horse, the more races they run, the more confident they get, and the more assured the performances are ... the more you do it, the better you get. But when they're only 3 years old, they have very little exposure to races."
- In the read: count a young horse's runs. A lightly raced 3yo (few career runs) is still learning and has more improvement left than his figures or his mark show; each run makes him more confident and his performances more assured. An exposed older horse on a rise has no such reserve.

## 2026-10-27 — YES (taught by him, after The Stony Baker 200/1, Catterick 12:40)

- Incident: The Stony Baker had two bad runs over 6f, the last at Southwell "soon led, headed over 2f out, weakened over 1f out". Dropped to 5f on a sharp track he won at 200/1, "prominent, took second before halfway ... ran on well, led inside final 110yds". I read only his figures (9th and 10th) and missed the early speed.
- His words: "often it's a great thing if a horse drops back in trip from 6 furlongs to 5 furlongs: they might have speed and they're going to get the trip as well. If they can break handy, stay in the pack, and kick for home early, they should get home."
- In the read: a horse that showed early pace and then faded over a longer trip, dropping back in trip, is a live contender whatever his finishing positions say. Read the comment for the speed ("soon led", "prominent", "chased leader", then "weakened late"), not the figure.

## 2026-10-27 — ASKED (the 50/50s: a closer over a handy horse)

- Incidents (27 Oct, all three where the winner was in my contenders): Catterick 13:10 By Jove Grace "towards rear ... ran on strongly ... took second final strides" behind Bladey Lady, who "made all ... cosily" over C&D last time and "Led ... rallied ... won going away". Wolverhampton 18:30 Midnight's Dream "towards rear ... stayed on well ... never nearer" behind Twitch, a C&D winner from the front rank, "Early pace, chased leaders ... led inside final furlong". Wolverhampton 19:00 Barry The Worm "towards rear ... ran on strongly, just failed" behind Territorial Star. Plus Just Golden, Inedit d'Amour and Ridger, all 2nd from the rear. On the day 25 of 30 winners raced on or near the pace; one came from the rear.
- What I missed: when two horses were close on form, I took the one whose comments said "ran on, never nearer", reading the late burst as the better horse. The winner was the one already in position.
- Question for William: **when two horses are close on form, should the read lean to the one whose comments show he races prominently or tracks the pace ("led", "prominent", "chased leaders"), and count "ran on ... never nearer" from the rear as a doubt rather than a strength, especially over sprint trips and on the all-weather? Yes or no.**
- **ANSWERED NO, 28 Oct, in his words:** "Race pace is different in every race. Depends on many factors: distance, going, track biases. I think this is a factor that you are weighting too heavy."
- In the read: no general lean to prominent racers. Pace is read race by race (the trip, the going, the track and its bias, who else wants to lead), as one question among many, never a day's statistic turned into a rule. One day's "25 of 30 on the pace" is a fact about 27 Oct, not a law.

## 2026-10-28 — YES (taught by him, the same night as the pace NO)

- His words: "Some jockeys are experts at dictating the pace of a race, and a good jockey can steal a race."
- Incidents it explains (27 Oct): Wolverhampton 17:00 Show Me Glory, Oisin Murphy, "Always led, restrained in lead, ridden to assert over 2f out, soon in command". Wolverhampton 17:30 Boy Basilica, Billy Loughnane, "Led narrowly, gradually lengthened and stretched field 3f out ... pushed steadily clear". Both races were won from the front by riders who set their own pace.
- In the read: in each race ask who is likely to lead, who is riding him, and whether he will be left alone. A top rider on a horse who can lead or sit handy, with no other pace in the race, can control the race and steal it from better-rated rivals. A question for each race, never a rule.

## 2026-10-28 — YES (taught by him, follows the jockey lesson)

- His words: "Also look at trainer jockey combos. But on all weather it is more important, as you can see the 2 jockeys you referred to are masters at it."
- Incidents (27 Oct, Wolverhampton, from the snapshot and results): Oisin Murphy rode 4 — Show Me Glory (Balding) WON, Fahidi (Balding) WON, Ridger (Sangster) 2nd, Zeuster (Morrison) 2nd on debut. Billy Loughnane rode 4 — Boy Basilica (Richard Hughes) WON, Territorial Star (Rebecca Menzies) WON, Midnight's Dream (Parr) 2nd, Macanudo (Ian Williams) 2nd. Four winners and four seconds from eight rides; I picked against both Balding–Murphy winners.
- In the read: look at who the trainer has put up, and whether the pair has a record together (the Racing API connector's get_trainer_jockey_analysis gives the pair's record; it costs nothing extra). On the all-weather weigh it more: the top AW riders (Murphy, Loughnane) control the pace and the race. A top rider booked by an in-form yard is a breadcrumb even when the figures are poor (Fahidi, form 63-405). A question for each race, never a rule.

## 2026-10-28 — YES (taught AGAIN: already in his rulebook, missed on 27 Oct)

- His words: "Also look at course and distance form. But remember I did ask: look at jockey form and trainer form. Like Oisin Murphy on a dreary night in Wolverhampton is not going there for the fun. Same as Billy. They have live chances and are riding horses with potential with big stables — another nuance and breadcrumb."
- ALREADY TAUGHT, the scar: the rulebook (src/racing_edge/study/morningread.py) has it twice — 3d, "the jockey BOOKING (a proper rider down for a small yard's one runner is intent)", and 4f, his law, "a stable in form, good jockey booking, and the market is positive, it often stacks up to a winner". On 27 Oct I read it every morning and still picked against Fahidi (Balding, Murphy, the exchange favourite) and Show Me Glory (Balding, Murphy). Course and distance form decided Bladey Lady, Eldeyaar, Twitch, Wobwobwob and Cabeza De Llave.
- In the read, every race: (1) C&D form, a win or a close run over this course and trip, written next to each contender; (2) the jockey's current form and the trainer's current form (last 14 days in the card); (3) THE BOOKING: a top rider at a small, dreary meeting is there because the ride has a live chance, usually from a big yard with a horse of potential. Each contender's line names all three. Questions, never gates.

## 2026-10-28 — YES (taught by him; the rulebook's 3d says it for the flat, now named for the all-weather)

- His words: "Also look at the draw on the all weather."
- Incidents (27 Oct, Wolverhampton, stall numbers from the snapshot): the nine winners were drawn 2, 3, 2, 9, 2, 5, 7, 8, 1, five of them in stalls 1–3. Midnight's Dream, drawn 11 of 11 at 7f, was "steadied start, towards rear ... never nearer" and beaten ¾l by Twitch from stall 2. Cabeza De Llave won the 5f from stall 1. One meeting, so a pointer, not a number.
- In the read, on the all-weather: write each contender's stall and ask what it means at this track and trip: can he get a position from it, will he be caught wide on the bends, does his run style suit the stall (a horse who needs to lead, drawn wide, has work to do). Weigh it most over the shorter trips. A question for each race, never a gate.
