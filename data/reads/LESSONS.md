## 2026-10-26 — YES (asked, answered the same night)

- Incidents: Siwa Oasis (Kempton 5:45) — I self-graded him NO BET at 13:15 because he had never won off his new mark (up 5lb to 82). He won at 2/1, "improved over 2f out ... kept on well". Fight Your Corner (Kempton 6:45) failed my 60-day race-fit line at 68 days and won at 4/1.
- The gap: both times I applied one line of the solid test strictly and overrode the stronger evidence beside it: the young-improver rule (3g-ii) and a C&D horse only just over the line.
- Question for William: **when a horse fails one part of the five-part solid test only narrowly (a race-fit 3yo improver up 5lb or less, or a course-and-distance horse a few days past 60), should the read weigh the whole case rather than rule him out on that one part? Yes or no.**
- **ANSWERED YES, 26 Oct, in his words:** "remember, 5lb rise. Remember I said to you about that? A three-year-old, that horse will be improving. He will only get better. So 5lb could be very, very doable. Look at his past victories. Look at the comments. Did he win well? Did he win going away? Was he eased down? These are all nuances that you can easily read and assess."
- In the read: a 3yo raised up to about 5lb after a win is not ruled out on "proven at mark". Read HOW he won. "Won going away", "eased down", "cosily", "readily", "easily" or "won well" mean more in hand than the margin, so the rise is likely within him. "All out", "just held on" or "driven out" mean little left. Weigh the whole case; never rule out on one narrow fail.
- His follow-up, same night: "it was there in black and white in his comment ... he was a horse getting better. A young horse, the more races they run, the more confident they get, and the more assured the performances are ... the more you do it, the better you get. But when they're only 3 years old, they have very little exposure to races."
- In the read: count a young horse's runs. A lightly raced 3yo (few career runs) is still learning and has more improvement left than his figures or his mark show; each run makes him more confident and his performances more assured. An exposed older horse on a rise has no such reserve.

## 2026-10-28 — YES (his breadcrumb list, taught item by item the night after day one)

- His words, in order: "Weight??" · "Also the going and class" · "Also form figures and days since last run" · "Also look at headgear and wind op" · "remember."
- With the lessons above (C&D form, jockey and trainer form, the booking, trainer-jockey combos, the jockey who dictates the pace, the draw on the all-weather, the money), these make THE BREADCRUMB LINE written under every contender in the morning read (docs/ROUTINES.md, step 4): C&D · trip · going · class · form figures · days since last run · last run (the comment) · headgear and wind op · weight and mark · jockey and trainer · pace · draw · the money.
- Why it exists, his word: "you knew all these breadcrumbs, and time and time again you don't use them." Read at the top and forgotten is the failure; asked of every contender is the cure. Questions, never gates.
- Added the same morning, his words: "Also look at trainer form at the course." On the line under JOCKEY AND TRAINER: the trainer's record at this course (get_trainer_course_analysis on the Racing API connector, no extra cost), beside his current form, and the jockey's course record (the rulebook's COURSE JOCKEY).
- And his caution, the same morning: "Remember we don't want to fall for past mistakes of hard rules everywhere ruling out most horses. These are more breadcrumbs. Think like me as you are evaluating a race with an open mind as you find the breadcrumbs." The list is where to look, never a test: no scores, no tick counts, no horse ruled out for a missing item. One loud breadcrumb can outweigh a tidy line.
- And on the market, the same morning: "Also look at the market, but remember you need to think like me, and not create hard rules, as this does not work. Horses are not binary machines; they need to be treated and evaluated like the animals they are." The money is one breadcrumb among the others (the line's last place to look, and the 11:21 pre-off check): read what it says about this horse today, beside his form, never a threshold that picks or rules out.
- And, the same morning: "Also look at the going on the day." The declared going at breakfast is not the going at the off: rain, drying wind, watering and going-stick readings change it, and non-runners follow. In the morning read, write the going as declared and each contender's form on that ground; at the 11:21 pre-off check, read the official going again and ask whom any change helps or hurts.
- And: "Also look at the jockey's form at the course." Beside the trainer's course record: the jockey's record at this course (get_jockey_course_analysis on the Racing API connector; the rulebook's COURSE JOCKEY). A rider who knows the track, its bends and where to be, is a breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's age." Read it with his other words: a young horse with few runs is still improving and learning (his 26 Oct YES: "the more races they run, the more confident they get"), so his figures and mark understate him; an older, exposed horse has shown his level, and a long losing run at his age says more. Weigh the weight-for-age allowance where 3yos meet older horses, and in 2yo races how much each has done. A breadcrumb read beside the form, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the trainer's record with the horse." How the horse has gone for THIS trainer: his runs and wins since he joined the yard (the trainer named on each past run in the snapshot's form), whether today is his first run for a new yard, how the trainer has placed him (dropped into the right grade, freshened, kept for this trip or ground), and the trainer's record with horses of this age (get_trainer_horse_age_analysis on the Racing API connector). A breadcrumb read beside the form, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the sire and dam." Pedigree matters most where the form is thin: a newcomer, a lightly raced 2yo or 3yo, or a horse trying a new trip, ground or surface. Ask what the sire's and dam's runners do at this trip and in this class (get_sire_distance_analysis, get_sire_class_analysis, get_dam_distance_analysis, get_dam_class_analysis, and the damsire's, on the Racing API connector), and whether the dam bred winners. For an exposed older horse his own form says more than his breeding. A breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's breeding for trip." Above all when the trip changes: a horse stepping up, is he bred to stay it (the sire's and damsire's runners at this distance, the dam's winners' trips)? A horse dropping back, is there speed in the pedigree? An unexposed horse at a new trip whose breeding says it suits him can improve past his form; one bred for the opposite has a question to answer. Read with his comments (a horse "staying on" at the end of a shorter trip, bred to stay, is the classic one). A breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's breeding for going." Most of all when a horse meets ground he has never raced on (first time on soft or heavy, or on the all-weather) or has thin form: does the pedigree say he will handle it? The Racing API connector has sire and dam records by trip and class but NOT by going, so say where the going read comes from: the dam's other runners' form on that ground where it can be found, or a sire's well-known preference, marked as general knowledge, never presented as a figure. A breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's breeding for surface." Turf against all-weather, and between the all-weather surfaces (Tapeta at Wolverhampton and Newcastle, Polytrack at Kempton, Lingfield and Chelmsford, Fibresand at Southwell): a horse on his first all-weather run, or switching surface, with thin form there. Does his family act on it? As with going, the connector has no sire or dam split by surface, so the read uses the dam's other runners on that surface where found, or a sire's well-known all-weather record marked as general knowledge, never a figure. A breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's breeding for class." For an unexposed horse, above all one stepping up in grade or running in a novice, conditions or pattern race: do the sire's and dam's runners win at this level (get_sire_class_analysis, get_dam_class_analysis, get_damsire_class_analysis on the Racing API connector), and is the dam's family a good one (black type, winners in good races)? A well-bred horse in a low grade, still unexposed, can be far better than his mark; a modestly bred one stepping into a hot race has a question to answer. With his law beside it: "class is permanent". A breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's breeding for age." Precocity and maturity: some families are quick and win as 2yos then level off; others are slow to come to hand and improve at 3, 4 and beyond (and over jumps, later still). A 2yo from a precocious family is ready now; a backward-bred 2yo or 3yo running green may be learning for later, or about to improve sharply; an older horse from a late-maturing family can still be on the up. The connector has no sire record by age, so the read uses the dam's other runners' ages when they won, or a sire's well-known profile marked as general knowledge, never a figure. A breadcrumb, never a rule.
- And: "Also look at the horse's breeding for headgear." Temperament runs in families: when a horse wears headgear for the first time, or is quirky (hangs, races keenly, finds little), ask whether his brothers and sisters out of the same dam improved for blinkers, a visor or a hood, where their form can be found. There is no data for this in the connector and little anywhere, so it is the faintest breadcrumb on the list: used only when it can be found, never guessed, never a figure, never a rule.
