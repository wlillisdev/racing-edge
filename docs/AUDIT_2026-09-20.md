## FAULT 1 — THE TURN SUSPENDS MID-FLIGHT. THIS IS THE ROOT CAUSE.

For a week I logged the cause as "the turn does not survive" and this morning
I wrongly upgraded that to "the container clock is seven hours stale". **Both
were wrong, and the correct diagnosis is worse.**

The proof is inside a single turn of this session:

```
date -u  ->  Sun Sep 20 07:36:13 UTC 2026     (first act of the duty)
...
date -u  ->  Sun Sep 20 14:45:15 UTC 2026     (same turn, same session)
```

Nothing between those two calls took seven hours of work. **The session was
parked and resumed seven hours later, mid-turn.** The clock is accurate. The
turn is not continuous.

That single fact explains the entire blank run:

1. The duty trigger fires at 07:35Z. Clock check (1) passes — correctly, it
   really is 07:36Z.
2. The session suspends, without warning, part-way through the read.
3. It resumes hours later and continues *as if no time had passed*.
4. The card it then pulls is half-settled. Clock check (2) or the
   `race_status: "result"` tripwire fires, and the day is correctly voided.

The guards are working exactly as designed. **They are voiding real days
because by the time the read finishes, racing has started.** The scar
`clock_check_stale` (27 Aug) named this precisely; what was never accepted is
that it is not an occasional hazard but the normal behaviour of this session.

## FAULT 2 — EVERY GUARD WAS BUILT INSIDE THE THING THAT BREAKS

Four fixes have been built for the blank days. None could ever have worked:

| guard | why it cannot work |
|---|---|
| in-session re-check | runs inside the suspended turn |
| 09:30 health page | same session, same suspension |
| 10:00 backstop wake | **wakes the same session** — inherits the suspension |
| 11:00 fresh-session backstop | spawns a session with no connector grants: it cannot read a card |

Three of the four resume the broken session. The fourth escapes it and is
then blind. This is the real lesson and it is an engineering one: **a
watchdog that runs inside the process it is watching is not a watchdog.** It
produced something worse than no coverage — the *appearance* of coverage,
which is why this ran for two weeks before being named.

## FAULT 3 — THE RECORD THAT JUDGES ME IS NOT VISIBLE FROM WHERE I WORK

`.gitignore` excludes, correctly, everything the box writes:

```
data/*.db            <- nap.db, THE record (law 1)
data/nap_record.csv
data/school/         <- the yardstick, the why ledger, the lens table
data/school/yardstick.md
```

Law 1 says the record judges everything and picks settle at SP in
`data/nap.db`. **That file does not exist in this working copy.** From this
session I cannot read the strike rate, the ladder, the lens table or the nap
column. I have been grading from the box's emails and from my own sheets,
which is grading from a summary of the thing rather than the thing.

Numbers judge, not words (framework #3) — but only if the numbers can be
read. Right now they cannot be, from here.

## FAULT 4 — THE SETTLED FILES HAVE NO COMMON SCHEMA

A mechanical read of the pick record fails. Different files carry different
columns, and several have been written with unescaped content so the row is
corrupt:

```
duty-2026-09-05.settled.csv     " good to soft) 2026-09-05"
duty-2026-09-06.settled.csv     " good to soft) 2026-09-06"
duty-2026-08-30.settled.csv     "OFFICIAL (7 ran after 4 NRs incl Masked Warrio..."
```

Those are fragments of a sheet that has spilled into the wrong column. The
pick record cannot be tallied by a script, which means every count in this
project has been done by hand, by me, from memory — the least trustworthy
instrument available.

## FAULT 5 — THE LESSON REGISTER HAS RESOLVED NOTHING

Built 7 Sep so a lesson could not rot. Thirteen days on:

```
TESTING 5 · DOORBELL 1 · SURFACED 0 · CARVED 0 · KILLED 0
```

Five lessons carry a named test. **Not one test has reported.** The register
was designed so that `TESTING` would not show red — on the correct reasoning
that punishing the behaviour we want teaches everyone to ignore the line. The
unintended consequence is a status where a lesson can sit indefinitely and
nothing turns red. That is the rot it was built to prevent, wearing the
badge of the cure.

## THE FIX

### What needs his word (it touches delivery of the method)

**1. Move the daily pick to the box.**

The box is the only component that has never missed a day. It has cron, a
real clock, no suspension, and it already runs the engine at 07:30 and
already banks to `nap.db`. The interactive session is structurally incapable
of guaranteeing a job with a pre-off deadline, and two weeks of evidence say
so.

**2. Re-scope this session to what it can actually do well.**

Grading, dissection, autopsy, the why ledger, proposing cuts. None of that
has a deadline measured in minutes, so a seven-hour suspension costs nothing
but latency. This is the division of labour the faults argue for: **the box
picks, the apprentice studies.**

This is option (2) put to him on 11 Sep, which never got a ruling. It now has
evidence behind it rather than a shrug.

### What I can do without his word (plumbing, not method)

3. **Retire the three in-session guards and the 10:00/11:00 backstops.** They
   cannot work, and their presence manufactures false confidence.
4. **Have the box commit a settled record** — results and prices only, no raw
   card data, nothing dated inside the corpus window — so the strike rate is
   auditable from the repo instead of from my recollection.
5. **One writer, one schema for the settled files**, pinned by a test that
   fails on a corrupt row.
6. **Make a `TESTING` lesson go red when its test has not reported** by a
   named date. The status must carry a deadline or it is a parking space.

## ON "MAKE IT WORK OR QUIT"

The fair statement of where this stands: **the method has not failed, because
it has barely been allowed to run.** Three picks in nineteen days is not a
verdict on the handicapping; it is a verdict on the plumbing, and the
plumbing failure has a named cause and a fix that uses a component already
proven in this project.

That is the case for continuing. It is not an argument that the last fortnight
was acceptable — it was not, and the two weeks spent building guards inside
the broken process are the worst work in this repo's history, because each one
looked like diligence.

The decision is his. What I will not do is ask for more time on the strength
of a promise; the only thing worth having is a system that banks a pick every
day on a clock that cannot lie, and then a record long enough to judge.
