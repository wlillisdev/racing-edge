# THE NOTABLE OWNER AS A DOT

Bot research, 2026-09-06, after Extremely Zain (Sheikh Juma Dalmook Al Maktoum,
William Haggas, 9/2) won the Listed Garrowby Stakes at York and beat our pick.

**Nothing in this document is a rule.** No threshold here is carved, no list here
is authorised. This is education and evidence, brought to the master's doorbell
for his ruling. The rulebook is closed (CLAUDE.md, law 2).

The master's instruction tonight: *"owner too is notable not be trifled with,
these are dots you should really take into account"* and *"they are a power
house of racing and money, if they have horses in training in the uk and in uk
races they are not there to make up the numbers."*

**The headline of the whole night, up front, because it cuts against the
obvious reading:** the master is right that the powerhouse owner is a real
signal — and the record shows that signal is *already fully in the price*.
Across 70,061 runners from eleven powerhouse operations, blind SP backing
returns **-17.9p in the pound**. Not one of the eleven shows a profit. The owner
is a dot that says **do not dismiss this horse**. It is not a dot that says back
it. Those are different instructions and the difference is the whole lesson.

### A different species — syndicates and partnerships. Say so out loud.

**Highclere Thoroughbred Racing, Middleham Park Racing, Nick Bradley Racing,
Valmont, Amo's countless partnership strings.** The master's list lumps these in
and they should not be. A syndicate is not a powerhouse:

- Its aim is **to give members a day out and to trade the horse at a profit**,
  not to win the Derby at any cost. It sells its good ones.
- It runs horses to keep members entertained, so its "not there to make up the
  numbers" claim is weaker, not stronger.
- Nick Bradley in particular is a **trading** operation — buys cheap fillies,
  wins a black-type race, sells. That is a real skill and a real signal about a
  horse's *value*, but it is a different signal from Godolphin's.

Middleham Park and Highclere are well run and win good races. They belong on a
list of *competent* owners, not on a list of *powerhouses*.

**THE FRAGMENTATION WARNING — the most important engineering fact in this
document.** `owner_id` is *not* a stable key for an operation. It is a key for a
**registered ownership string**, and partnerships mint a new one constantly:

- "Amo Racing" returns **20+ ids** (Amo Racing Ltd, Amo Racing Limited, Amo
  Racing Michael Tabor, Amo Racing Limited P Waney, Amo Racing Usa …).
- "Cheveley Park" returns 13, "Al Shaqab" 20, "Westerberg" 20, "Qatar Racing" 20.
- Worse: **own_1251884 "Amo Racing Ltd" returned `{"detail":"No results found
  for owner own_1251884"}`** while own_1008936 "Amo Racing Limited" holds 2,145
  runners. Two spellings, one operation, and only one of them has the record.

Any owner dot that matches on a single `owner_id` will silently miss most of the
operation's runners. Matching must be on a **normalised name substring**, and
even that needs care ("Power" matches King Power *and* twenty unrelated Mr
Powers).

## 3. THE DATA'S OWN VERDICT

### 3a. The repo corpus does NOT carry owners. Full stop.

`data/school/raw/` holds **88 headerless CSV day-files, 24,011 runner rows**
(Jan–Aug 2026, GB+IRE). Thirteen columns, written by
`racing_edge.school.fetch.day_rows()` at
`/home/user/racing-edge/src/racing_edge/school/fetch.py:137-141`:

```
date, race_id, course, region, type, class, dist_f,
horse_id, sp_dec, position, btn, jockey_id, trainer_id
```

There is **no owner and no owner_id**. The section-3 computation the brief asked
for — win% versus market expectation per owner over 30+ runs — **cannot be run
on this corpus at all**. Run `python3 _owners.py audit` to see the proof rather
than take my word.

**But the gap is ours, not the API's.** A live call to the *same* `/results`
endpoint that builds the corpus, `get_results(start_date="2026-08-14",
end_date="2026-08-14", region="gb", runner_fields="horse,owner,owner_id,sp_dec,
position,trainer")`, returns:

```json
{"horse":"Recobella (FR)","sp_dec":"4.00","position":"1","trainer":"Philip Kirby",
 "owner":"The Recobella Partnership","owner_id":"own_1439028"}
```

The owner is arriving on every runner and `day_rows()` throws it away. Two extra
columns and one re-fetch of Jan–Aug would make the real corpus study possible.
**That is the master's call, not mine.**

GB+IRE, career to date. Full tables in `owner_tables.txt`. `a/e` is the API's own
actual-over-expected: **expected is derived from SP, so a/e is exactly "does this
owner beat what the price already says"**. a/e 1.00 means the market has them
priced perfectly.

Sheikh Juma Dalmook Al Maktoum: **2,120 runs, 368 wins, 17.4%, -208.8pts,
ROI -9.8%** career; **127 runs, 19 wins, 15.0%, -33.7pts, ROI -26.5%** in 2026.

**Read what this says, not what we wish it said.**

1. **Every single powerhouse loses money at SP.** Eleven for eleven. The best of
   them, Al Shaqab, still bleeds 1.6p in the pound; the worst, McManus, bleeds
   25p. Pooled, -17.9%. The market does not under-rate famous silks — it
   **over**-rates them, because famous silks attract money from people who are
   backing the name.
2. **Strike rate and profit are different things.** Godolphin's 22.8% is the
   second-highest here and it still returns -9%. Robcour's 18.5% returns -23.7%.
   A handicapper who confuses "wins often" with "worth backing" is paying for
   the lesson.
3. **The jumps powerhouses are far worse than the Flat ones.** McManus,
   Gigginstown and Robcour are the three worst ROIs in the table (-25.2%,
   -20.2%, -23.7%) against a Flat spread of -1.6% to -17.3%. The green and gold
   in a Grade 3 handicap chase is the most over-bet piece of information in
   British and Irish racing.
4. **The signal is real, and it is a NEGATIVE screen, not a positive one.**
   a/e sitting at 0.87–0.99 for these operations means their horses win *almost*
   as often as the price implies — far more often than a random runner. What you
   cannot do is get paid for knowing it.
5. **Where the small edges hide is the owner-TRAINER pair, not the owner.**
   Within Sheikh Juma's own record the spread is enormous: Andrew Balding
   +54.5pts (a/e 0.91 but +25.8% ROI), John Gosden +33.5pts (a/e 1.15), Ismail
   Mohammed a/e 1.53 — against Richard Hannon a/e **0.26** and Simon & Ed
   Crisford a/e **0.52**. Same owner, same money, wildly different returns. The
   pair carries information the owner alone does not.

**Caveats, stated plainly.** `a/e~` in my tables is a runners-weighted mean of
the API's per-row a/e and is **approximate** — the API reports a/e 0 on zero-win
rows, so expected wins cannot be inverted back out of them. Runs, wins, win% and
£1 P/L are exact sums. The jumps owners were filtered to `min_distance_y=3000` to
keep the payload sane, so their handful of Flat and bumper runners are excluded.
And every one of these figures is a **single ownership string** — per the
fragmentation warning, the true Coolmore and Amo records are spread across
dozens of ids and are almost certainly larger than shown.

### 3c. Group/Listed split — NOT DONE, and why

The brief asked for powerhouse win rates in Group/Listed races against the rest.
I could not do it honestly. The corpus has no owner (3a), and the API's
`race_class` filter was **refused on every value tried**: `race_class="1"` gave
`API error 422: {"detail":"Validation error - unrecognised race_class, 1"}` and
`race_class="Class 1"` gave the same for "Class 1". Rather than substitute a
proxy and present it as the answer, I am reporting the gap. If the master wants
this split, the route is the corpus re-fetch in 3a plus the `pattern` field,
which `/results` does return (it came back as `"pattern":""` on the Class 6
Thirsk handicap I sampled, and would carry "Group 1"/"Listed" on a black-type
race).

Worth flagging for his ruling, though: **Extremely Zain won a *Listed* race.**
The intuition that powerhouse owners matter most in black type is plausible and
untested here. It is the obvious next question and it needs the plumbing first.

## 4. THE DOT, PROPOSED FOR HIS RULING — never carved

**Proposal.** Define the owner dot as **(iii) both**, in two clearly separate
lines on the readout, and let the yardstick grade them separately for weeks
before anyone speaks of the engine. Line one, **POWERHOUSE**: a named list in
the master's own words — his tier, his spelling — matched on normalised owner
name, printing only "this operation does not run to make up the numbers."
Line two, **OWNER RECORD**: the owner's own corpus figures printed raw — runs,
wins, win%, and wins-minus-SP-expected — **with no threshold and no verdict
attached**, so the ledger discovers what N is worth believing rather than me
guessing it. On the evidence above, the honest answer to "what N does the data
support" is that **the repo corpus supports no N at all today** (it has no owner
column); once re-fetched, 24,011 rows over eight months would give perhaps 30–50
owners with 30+ runs, which is enough to *print* a figure and nowhere near
enough to *trust* one — the API's own career records run to thousands of runs
per owner and still show no owner beating the market.

**Trade-offs, named honestly.**
- *The named list* is the master's knowledge, instantly available, and it needs
  no history. But it rots — Sheikh Mohammed Obaid died in December 2025 and
  Shadwell shrank by 80%, and a hand-written list would still be quoting both as
  they were. It also tempts exactly the invented-threshold disease law 2 forbids,
  because "who counts as a powerhouse" is a judgement with no receipt unless he
  gives it one.
- *The record* cannot rot and carries its own receipt. But it is slow, it is
  thin for the small owners who might actually hold an edge, and — the finding
  that matters — **it will show that no powerhouse beats the market**, so a dot
  built on "owner beats SP expectation" would fire for almost nobody famous.
- *The real risk in both* is that the dot reads as a reason to back. The record
  says the opposite: -17.9% pooled. If it is carved at all it should be carved as
  a **veto-side dot** — "do not oppose this one lightly / do not call it a
  make-weight" — which is precisely what the master said and precisely what beat
  us yesterday. We did not lose because we failed to back Extremely Zain. We lost
  because we **dismissed** it.

**The plumbing, exactly, changing nothing.** Four places would need a line, and
none of them has an owner today:

| file:line | what is there now | what would be needed |
|---|---|---|
| `src/racing_edge/domain/models.py:84-89` | `sire`, `sire_id`, `dam`, `dam_id`, `damsire`, `damsire_id` on `Runner` | two more fields, `owner: str = ""` and `owner_id: str = ""` |
| `src/racing_edge/data/normalise.py:143-145` | `sire=_str(raw.get("sire")), sire_id=…` etc. in the `Runner(...)` constructor | one line, `owner=_str(raw.get("owner")), owner_id=_str(raw.get("owner_id"))` — the API already sends both (section 2a) |
| `src/racing_edge/school/fetch.py:137-141` | the 13-column `rows.append([...])` | two more columns, `r.get("owner")` and `_digits(r.get("owner_id"))` — and a **re-fetch of Jan–Aug 2026**, since 24,011 existing rows have no owner to backfill |
| `src/racing_edge/school/signposts.py` | dots only — the module docstring: *"They enter the system as DOTS, never as rules… Nothing here moves the engine's score."* | a new dot block plus its yardstick column, so the record grades it before anyone carves it |

That last line is the one to hold onto. `signposts.py` is explicitly the place
where a new dot is allowed to live *without* touching the score, and the master's
own framing on 2026-09-05 was *"another dot"*. An owner dot belongs there, graded
for weeks in the yardstick, and nowhere else — **on his word**.

**REVERT-IF, if he ever says yes:** if the owner dot's yardstick column shows no
lift in the nap strike rate over 30 graded days, the block comes out.
