# VISION TEST — the new doctrine backtested on our own results (2026-08-22)

Question (the master: "test your new vision on own results, see how its
calibrated"). Claim under test: bet only fingerprint races; the winner
lives in the market's top two; the skill is the twin choice at ranks 2-3.

Method: 2,727 stored races (data/school/raw, Jan–Aug 2026). Fingerprint
proxy: GB flat turf (AW-course list excluded), Class 3-4, <=11 priced
runners, top-3 SP concentration > 0.75. Level stakes at SP, month-split.
No leakage: race-level SP/position facts only.

## Results

| bet | n | strike% | ROI% | months positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fav, ALL races | 2673 | 36.7 | -5.3 | 1/8 |
| fav, FINGERPRINT races | 262 | 46.9 | **-3.1** | 5/8 (Mar-Jul all +, Aug -21) |
| rank-2 blind, fingerprint | 262 | 22.9 | -22.0 | 3/8 |
| rank-3 blind, fingerprint | 262 | 14.5 | -17.4 | 3/8 |
| ranks 2+3 both, fingerprint | 524 | 18.7 | -19.7 | 3/8 |

Winner-in-top-2 in fingerprint races: 69.8%. Top-4: 93.5%.

## Verdict — half right, half wrong, both halves now receipted

CONFIRMED: the race filter works. Fingerprint races are soluble — the
favourite strikes 46.9% there (vs 33.9% baseline) and loses only 3.1p/£
blind, month-positive March through July. Race selection alone is worth
~8 points of ROI over betting everywhere.

REFUTED: the twin theory BLIND. Ranks 2-3 in these races are heavily
over-bet — blind they lose 17-22p/£, far worse than the fav. "The
reading premium lives at ranks 2-3" survives only as a claim about
READS, never as a default: taking the fav's rival requires an earned,
named reason, because the blind version of that bet is the worst one
available in our best races.

THE BENCHMARK THIS SETS: in a betting race the favourite at -3.1% is
the bar. Every pick against the fav must beat it or the record says
follow the market in our races. The TWIN CHOICE gauge and the
engine-bet column (PR #65) exist to answer exactly this within 50
picks. n=262 is past the 50-race judge bar, short of the 800 rule bar:
a candidate to keep measuring, not a law.
