# THE CANDIDATE LIST — written BEFORE the replay runs

Committed 2026-09-07, before a single replay race has been scored. That is
the whole point of the page.

The master, 2026-09-07: "can we not run the same set of criteria and lenses
on our set of results and keep fine tuning it until we get a better win
percentage?" Yes. But the number of things we try is the thing that decides
whether the winner is a finding or an accident, so it is fixed here, in
advance, where it can be checked against what we later claim.

## WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS

If you score thirty rules and report the best one, the best one is roughly
two standard errors above the benchmark by arithmetic alone, whether or not
any of them work. On 100 races that is about ten percentage points of pure
noise wearing a rosette. Every tie-break this project has invented — most
reasons, the class line, the mark — was found that way: after the fact, on
the week that suggested it, with no count of how many were tried.

So: the list is closed before the data is seen, and the report must state
how many were tried.

## THE BAR A RULE MUST CLEAR

All four, or it is not reported as a finding:

1. **50 decided pairs minimum** in the cell (his law: judge nothing under 50).
2. **Beats the benchmark on the tuned half AND on the held-out half.** One
   half is a coin toss with two sides.
3. **Beats it on strike AND on return.** Strike alone flatters the shorter
   horse; return alone flatters the longer. His correction, 07 Sep.
4. **Survives the month test** (tier0.month_test, his own bar, already coded).

## THE STOPPING RULE

If nothing clears the bar, the report says so and the hunt for a mechanical
tie-break ends. That is a real possible answer and it is worth knowing: it
would mean the final call is not a rule we are missing but a judgement the
record cannot mechanise, and the money would be in choosing which races to
enter rather than which of two horses to name.

Adding an eleventh candidate later is allowed, on his word, and the report
must then say eleven were tried, not ten.
