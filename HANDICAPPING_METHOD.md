# The Method — how we read a race

The single source of truth for *how the system should pick*. Your handicapping
nuances + the old-school pro read, as one method. The numeric score is a sanity
check — **this** is the engine.

Each line is tagged:
- **[S]** cheap, runs on the **Standard** card
- **[P]** needs **Pro** (per-horse history or breeding analysis)
- **[us]** built from our own data over time (results/snapshots we collect)

The read is a **case you build**: stack the positives, weigh the one big
negative, and the **price has to be in your favour**. Most races are a no-bet.

## Core principle — a race is a race
**Read every race. Pass at the *race* level, never the *category* level.**
There are no excluded race types, classes, or grades. "Some races are worse than
others" is true — but you still *look*, because a blanket exclusion just leaves
your own winners behind. The category filters that lived in the old quant
(jumps, Class 3, "Unknown") existed to patch a placer-finding picker — they are
scaffolding to be removed once the *read* does the picking, not handicapping.

**The greats start humble.** Every champion began as a maiden / bumper /
point-to-pointer — unexposed, no figures, cheap to be on. *Best Mate* was a
point-to-pointer bought for a few hundred; a category-exclusion system would
never have looked at him. The "lowly" races are where breeding + intent earn
their keep and where the sharp money gets on *first* — so they are exactly the
races to read, not skip.

## 1. Is the form even worth a damn?  (does it lie?)
- What did it beat, what beat it — and has that race **worked out** (have horses
  from that field won *since*)? A close 3rd in a race that's franked beats a cosy
  win over nothing.  **[us]** (race-quality cache, sharper over time) · deeper **[P]**

## 2. Read the running, not the result
- Won easily with more in hand vs got every yard. Beaten horse met trouble —
  short of room, wide, slow away, ran green. **The run that was better than the
  finishing position** is the note you keep.  comments **[S]** · full detail **[P]**

## 3. Form trajectory  (read the curve, not the figures)
- **Improving `5-3-2-3`** → coming to the boil, "needs the right race" → **BACK**
- **Bottler `2-2-2-2`** → perennial placer, won't get its head in front →
  **AVOID — unless it drops in class** (then it's finally superior enough)  **[S]**

## 4. Where is it with the handicapper?  (weight & mark)
- **Ahead of the mark** (climbing the weights but *still winning*) = keep backing.
- **Well-in AND ahead of mark** = live winner.  **Well-in but topped-out** (only
  won *below* today's class) = placer, leave it.
- **Weight:** career-low weight (+), top-weight = at his ceiling (−), pounds
  dropped vs last run (+) — **1 length ≈ 1 lb**. A win to defy a penalty/raise.
  weight off card **[S]** · proven-at-level / class trajectory **[P]**

## 5. Will today suit — really?  (conditions: ground first)
- **GROUND — a front-line filter, not a footnote.** Proven on the going → strong
  tick; all its form on the opposite / never handled it → strong caution. Half of
  all form is a lie until the going is right.
- **Trip** — proven, or stepping up/down; does it stay, or want shorter (how it
  finishes tells you).
- **Course** — horses-for-courses: sharp vs galloping vs stiff finish.
- **Class** — proven at this level vs stepping up on a flattering win.
- **Handedness** — left- vs right-handed (course is a fixed fact; the horse's
  record by hand needs history).
- **Seasonal** — some come good spring / midsummer / autumn.
  today's conditions **[S]** · the horse's *relationship* to them **[P]**

## 6. Breeding  (the page IS the form for the unexposed)
- Debutant / once-raced / first time at a new trip — no form, so read the
  pedigree: speed vs stamina, optimal trip, **ground aptitude**, precocity.
  By a sprinter out of a staying mare = should get a trip; damside screams soft;
  half to a Group horse.  names **[S]** · aptitude (sire/dam distance/class/going) **[P]**

## 7. Fitness & intent  (the "connections have gone for it" stack)
- **Fitness** — fresh and ready vs needs the run after a break (first run back can
  be the prep, beware the bounce).
- **Yard in form** — cold stables don't win however good the horse looks. **[S]**
- **Changes are opportunities, not risks:**
  - **Wind op** → POSITIVE (it ran bad because it couldn't breathe; now it can). **[S]**
  - **Stable switch** → potential bounce (fresh eyes), stronger to an in-form yard. **[S]**
  - **First-time headgear** → neutral; trainer addressing a problem — positive only
    when it *stacks* with other intent. **[S]**
- **THE INTENT STACK** (this is where the winners hide):
  - **Longest traveller** — yard ships it a long way past nearer tracks = they fancy it.
  - **Local trainer + course record** — yard next to the track, schools there,
    knows every blade = an edge others don't have.  (one distance gives both) **[S, cached]**
  - **Jockey intent** — a top rider with a single booking / travelling to an
    evening card for ONE ride; and the big one: the **stable jockey's PICK** —
    when Townend is on Mullins' *second or third string*, that one is screaming. **[S + pairings]**
  - **Consistent gamble** — steady, one-way money all morning (not a late blip). **[us, snapshots]**

## 8. The market & value
- **Steamer / consistent gamble** = confidence. **Drifter** in a yard you'd
  expect to be confident = warning, leave it.
- **Value is the whole game** — a bigger price than its true chance. No value, no
  bet, even if it wins.  **[us, snapshots]**

## 9. Pace & shape
- Where's the pace? Lone front-runner in a muddling race can steal it; a hold-up
  horse needs a strong gallop and a clear run. Draw bias at the right track/trip.
  partial **[S]**

## 10. When to PASS  (the discipline half)
Actively hunt reasons *against*. Leave it when:
- bottler not dropping in class · flattered winner (beat nothing / soft lead) ·
  up in class **and** trip at once · well-in but actually *declining* · ground
  wrong · no value.  **Most races are a no-bet.**

## The synthesis
Not points added — a **case built**: "won well last time in a race that's working
out, 3lb well-in and still progressive, proven on today's ground and round this
stiff track, yard's flying, jockey's chosen it over the favourite, drawn to get a
lead to aim at — and it's 9/2 when it should be 3s." Positives stacked, no glaring
hole, **price in our favour → bet.** Anything less → wait.

---

## The learning loop — the post-race autopsy
The habit that sharpens the read: **after every race, go to the winner's form
and work out why we didn't have it.** Not "we lost" — *why*. For each race the
NAP didn't win, read the actual winner with open eyes and answer one question:

> **Which line of the method would have flagged this winner — and did we have it?**

- **Had the line, ignored it** → a *weighting* fault in the build (fix the code).
- **Didn't have the line** → a *new signal* to add to the method.

Done by hand it sharpens one punter; done by the machine on **every** race it
finds the pattern across hundreds of misses no memory could hold — "we keep
passing gambled travellers", "we keep missing improvers dropping in class." That
is where the analysis earns its place: not inventing edges, but telling us **what
the read is consistently missing.** Discipline as ever — the autopsy records the
*handicapping reason* the winner won, never a bare stat. (Engine: the winner-
learning loop, aimed at our misses; feeds back into this document.)

## What this means for Pro vs Standard
- **Standard runs the INTENT cluster** — traveller, local trainer, jockey intent,
  gamble, hot yard, headgear, stable switch, **form trajectory** (improver /
  bottler off the string). This is what put a winner up.
- **Pro unlocks the DEEP READ** — ground aptitude, breeding, proven conditions,
  class trajectory, seasonal, handedness, form-franking depth. This is most of the
  *craft*.

The honest fork: **deep read (Pro) vs intent-only (Standard).** Everything we
build next hangs on that one call.
